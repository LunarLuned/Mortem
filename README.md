# MORTEM

### A fabric mod creating a 'soft conversion' of Minecraft; adding a harder challenge to the game while touching up on many things left behind and forgotten.

This mod has 3 main missions under it's belt that it works to achieve.
* Going back and giving as many disregarded items and blocks uses as possible
* Making the game feel more fresh to veteran or consistent players by changing a good chunk of the gameplay while not being too intrusive
* Making the game harder while still being fair, unlike most other 'conversion' mods I've played in my time.

The fourth mission is a bit bigger and broader of one. I plan on 'converting' every dimension in the game in Mortem. The Overworld has been a major focus for a while now and right now with the release of 1.0.0, the nether is in my new sights for a 1.1.0 release. My goal is to make everything more fair, challenging and fun for even veteran players.

### Why structure the mod like this?

Something that really means a lot to me as both a player and a modder is how mods are split up nowadays. That is not necessarily a bad thing but I do kind of miss the giant mods where it was just everything the creator wanted to do in one file. That may be why Mortem is the way it is, instead of being one mod for each major overhaul. (Especially since this mod already has over [~150 new changes](https://github.com/LunarLuned/Mortem/wiki/Changelog))

### What's in the mod then?

Way too many things for me to put here. You can read the entire changelog of the entire mod (so far) [here](https://github.com/LunarLuned/Mortem/wiki/Changelog), but to include a few of my favorite changes:
* Each hostile nightly mob has had lots of changes to make each one have their own new twist
* So many items from the past getting their own new uses, even as simple as crafting recipes
  * Such as Charcoal being able to be used to create gunpowder with sand or potent sulfur
  * Such as Campfires being much more useful early game, being your saving grace when your natural healing is lacking without many high saturation foods, or high-producing charcoal.
  * Such as Resin being able to repair all metal items, similar to how amber was used to repair many valuable ceramics in the past.
* The gameplay systems that be have been overhauled to be more challenging while not being brainshatteringly exhausting (regeneration, material progression, etc)
* Many generally 'stupid' systems have been reworked or changed entirely (Anvils and how EXP work, certain enchantments and how they were just kind of bandaid fixes, Milk)
  * Mending for example, is an enchantment solely needed because anvils make items cost too much after repairing too many times, and durability is unforgiving. Enchanted items for example NOLONGER BREAK, but instead become unusable at around 3 durability, allowing you to go repair them at the NEW AND IMPROVED ANVIL that doesnt have XP scaling; meaning repairing a diamond helmet is as good as 3 diamonds and 3 levels, always.
* Bosses are actively getting major overhauls to be more challenging and FUN. (Wither has two new attacks with a bunch of changes applied, soon to be the same with the Dragon)
* Certain farms, loot in structures and other 'busted' ways of getting early game loot being nerfed.
* Fire being much more of a threat and Weakness multiplying the damage you can take
* ... and so much more! And that's only 1.0.0!

### Why should I care?

I want people to know that Minecraft is an amazing game, and I really think that a lot of people's issues is that they tend to play so long that they can see all of the flaws it has, or they get too used to the game that it becomes boring for them; forming that mentality of the dreaded '2 week period'. I want Mortem to make Minecraft feel like a different game while still being the same game. A friend had told me that Mortem felt like a "difficulty gamemode thats just higher than hard, like what hardcore should be", and I suppose that if there was anyway to describe it, it's that; Mortem is just a gamemode for Minecraft. I hope that you can play it and enjoy the vision I had as much as I am.
